# SMKH Privacy Request Handling Procedure

**Owner:** Workside Software

**Status:** Internal operational procedure

**Effective:** November 9, 2026
**Scope:** SellMyKernHome.com website inquiries and related SMKH administrative records

This procedure is operational groundwork, not a determination that the California Consumer Privacy Act applies. Legal deadlines, exceptions, and required disclosures must be confirmed for the request and the business's applicable obligations.

## 1. Intake

Accept a request submitted through the existing SMKH contact route, a reply to an authorized first-party communication, or the public SMKH telephone number. The requester should identify the request as one or more of:

- access or information request;
- correction request;
- deletion request;
- do-not-contact or marketing withdrawal;
- question or complaint about website data handling.

Do not ask the requester to submit passwords, financial records, government identification, or unrelated sensitive documents through the seller form.

## 2. Tracking

An authorized SMKH administrator should record:

- the request category and date received;
- the existing lead reference, when one is already available;
- the minimum contact information needed to respond;
- verification steps and result without copying unnecessary identity documents;
- actions taken, systems checked, exceptions considered, and date completed;
- the responder and any legal escalation.

Do not place request details in analytics, URLs, public logs, or notification emails.

## 3. Identity and authority verification

Use information already associated with the inquiry where practical. Verification must be proportionate to the sensitivity of the requested action. Do not disclose seller or property information merely because a requester knows a property address.

Escalate before acting when:

- the requester cannot be reasonably matched to the record;
- the request concerns another person, estate, trust, business, or authorized agent;
- disclosure could expose another person's information;
- deletion could conflict with legal, security, fraud-prevention, audit, or dispute-preservation needs.

## 4. Request handling

### Access or information

Identify relevant SMKH lead, consent, audit, and notification records. Exclude secrets, internal security data, and information about other people. Obtain legal review when scope or required format is uncertain.

### Correction

Record the requested correction and the source of the request. Preserve audit history rather than silently rewriting prior events.

### Deletion

Determine which records can be deleted or de-identified and which must be retained for an applicable exception. Never use a destructive database operation without an approved, record-specific plan and backup. Preserve the fact that a do-not-contact request exists when needed to honor it.

### Do-not-contact or marketing withdrawal

Record the request promptly in the authorized SMKH workflow. Do not treat deletion of the main lead as a substitute for preserving a minimal suppression preference. Marketing automation and partner sharing are not currently enabled.

## 5. Response and escalation

- Acknowledge receipt through the requester's verified channel.
- Do not promise a statutory deadline until applicability is confirmed.
- Escalate legal questions, disputed identity, third-party requests, threatened claims, law-enforcement demands, and preservation obligations.
- State accurately what was completed, what was retained, and why; do not claim deletion from a processor until verified.

## 6. Audit evidence

Retain only the minimum evidence needed to show receipt, verification, decision, actions, and response. Access must remain limited to authorized SMKH administrators. Do not send request evidence to external buyers, brokers, GNZ, Miramar, or other unapproved recipients.

## 7. Periodic review

Review this procedure when the privacy notice, consent version, service providers, telephone processing, external sharing plan, or applicable law changes. Workside should document CCPA applicability facts separately rather than assuming coverage or exemption.
